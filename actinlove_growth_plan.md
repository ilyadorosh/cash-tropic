# ActInLove Growth Plan & Station F Prep

*Prepared 2026-08-14*

## 1. Where things stand

actinlove.com (built by BLØB.haus, currently v2.14.0) lets two people generate a shared, AI-powered "connection page" at a custom URL — think a living page for a relationship, with a chat feature, a "constellation" social feed of created connections, an ambient music player, and small gamified nudges toward acts of kindness ("love compounds in tiny deposits"). It's a working MVP already live on the web, which matters a lot for what follows.

## 2. Growth marketing plan

The product's core loop is inherently viral: one person creates a page, the other person has to see it to complete the experience. That's the lever to pull hardest before spending on anything else.

**Sharpen the invite loop.** Make sure the moment someone receives a connection page is as frictionless and emotionally strong as possible — no login wall before they can see it, a clear "make your own" prompt right after they're moved by it, and an easy way to reshare to a third person (tag a friend, start a new page for someone else). Track this loop's conversion rate (pages created → pages opened by the recipient → recipient creates their own) before doing any paid acquisition; it's the number that determines whether paid spend will ever pay back.

**Content that rides the "act in love" philosophy.** The site's own framing — small deliberate gestures over grand ones — is strong, shareable content territory: short-form video (TikTok/Reels/Shorts) built around one tiny gesture per post ("call your mom today," "text the friend you've been meaning to text"), each ending with the actinlove.com page as the call-to-action. This is low-cost and matches organic discovery patterns for relationship/wellness apps far better than traditional ads.

**Community and press angle, not just app-store discovery.** Relationship and connection apps get real traction from being featured in newsletters and communities that already care about intentional relationships — Hinge/Lasting-style relationship coaches, couples-therapy Instagram accounts, "slow living" and wellness newsletters, and Product Hunt / Betalist-style launch communities for the tech-forward audience. A Product Hunt launch in particular is worth timing deliberately (mid-week, with a handful of committed upvoters lined up in advance) since it can be a single high-leverage day rather than an ongoing cost.

**Paid, only after the loop is validated.** Once the invite-to-creation conversion rate above is healthy, small-budget paid tests on Meta/TikTok targeted at "relationship goals," "couples," "long-distance relationship," and similar interest clusters tend to be the cheapest entry point for this category — but this should come after the organic loop, not before, since paid traffic into a weak loop just burns budget.

**Track one metric religiously.** For a connection-based product, the north star isn't visits — it's completed connections (both people engaged with the page). Everything above should be evaluated against whether it moves that number.

## 3. Station F: which program fits, and how to prepare

Station F (Paris) runs several distinct programs, and picking the right one matters more than the marketing pitch itself:

| Program | Focus | Cost | Notes for actinlove |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Founders Program** | Early-stage tech startups, any sector | From €259/desk/month | Best general fit — rolling admissions, hybrid, 3–18 months |
| **F/ai** | AI-specific startups targeting €1M revenue in 6 months | — | Only worth it if you can credibly project fast, AI-driven revenue; next batch Sept 15, 2026, **applications currently closed** |
| **Fighters Program** | Founders from underrepresented backgrounds | Free | Worth checking if you qualify — free is free; deadline Oct 18, 2026; French-language |
| **Landing Zone** | Low-commitment way to explore Station F | €269/desk/month | A softer entry point if you're not ready to commit to Founders Program yet |

Given actinlove already has a live MVP, the **Founders Program** is the most natural target — it explicitly wants a working product over slide decks, which you already have.

**What Station F actually screens for**, based on their own guidance (roughly 4,000 applications/year, ~6% accepted):

- A real, demoable MVP — not mockups. Have a short screen-recorded walkthrough ready, not just a live link, in case reviewers can't easily replicate the two-person flow themselves.
- A dedicated, full-time founding team (they explicitly deprioritize solo or part-time founders) — be ready to speak to who's committing full-time and any prior history working together.
- Plain language over buzzwords — they specifically call out avoiding "AI-powered" as a selling point in itself; describe what the product does and why it matters instead.
- Honest metrics — connections created, recipient open rate, repeat usage, anything you have, even if small. Confidentiality is respected, but omissions read worse than modest numbers.
- A warm introduction if possible — a recommendation from a current Founders Program resident, VC, or Selection Board member measurably helps; worth a short outreach push on LinkedIn before applying.

## 4. De-fragmenting the workflow

The recurring complaint under all of this seems to be that marketing, feedback, and the application process are scattered across too many disconnected tools. A few concrete ways to pull that together:

- **One tracker, one source of truth.** A single spreadsheet or lightweight board (channel → action → status → metric) for the growth plan above, so marketing activity isn't split across notes, chats, and memory. I can build this as an actual tracked spreadsheet if useful.
- **Connect the tools you're already using.** If your social, analytics, or project-management tools have a Cowork connector (Slack, Notion, Google Drive, etc.), connecting them here means I can pull real numbers instead of you copy-pasting between apps — worth doing before this plan needs its first update.
- **One doc for Station F**, not scattered notes — the application, the demo video link, the metrics snapshot, and the warm-intro outreach list all in one place, updated as you go rather than reconstructed each time.
