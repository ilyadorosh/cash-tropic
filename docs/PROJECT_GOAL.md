# Project Goal

Action in Love is a game-shaped interface for AI, memory, and physics.

The game is the primary surface. Chat is the operating layer. Love letters are not the whole product; they are one emotional protocol inside the world. A generated page can become a message, a memory object, a gift, a mission clue, a relationship state, or something an NPC remembers later.

## Product Thesis

People should be able to walk through their own thinking.

Instead of settings living in a flat panel, the game can make them spatial and visible:

- model choice as places, machines, or characters
- temperature as literal heat, volatility, or weather
- memory as locations, traces, archives, and relationships
- prompts as rituals, tools, books, or mission scripts
- NCA and KAM demos as living educational systems

The app should help people do stable work. It should make model behavior easier to feel, compare, and control.

## Technical North Star

The long-term thread is energy and computation:

- memory layers that make AI interaction persistent
- browser-native simulations that teach stability and dynamics
- neural cellular automata as living systems
- KAM and physics demos as intuition builders
- model controls that expose temperature, uncertainty, and behavior
- heat, dissipation, and Landauer-limit thinking as the deeper research direction

This does not need to be solemn. The town can have jokes, police, churches, pedestrians, missions, strange machines, and living terrain. The point is that the playful layer makes the difficult layer easier to touch.

## Current Focus

The near-term product should make the world feel less lonely and more useful:

- pedestrians from database or Redis memory
- building names, icons, and interactable meaning
- a visible HUD with time, weather, money, model, and mode
- police and mission interactions that do something legible
- chat as the deeper action layer
- love-letter pages integrated into NPCs, missions, and memories

## Contributor Shape

Good contributions make one part of the world more alive or one concept easier to understand.

Examples:

- connect saved characters to 3D pedestrians
- show names and icons over buildings
- make model parameters visible in the HUD
- turn generated letters into game objects or dialogue memories
- stabilize mission markers and interaction prompts
- make NCA/KAM demos teach through play
- document one system clearly enough that someone else can extend it
